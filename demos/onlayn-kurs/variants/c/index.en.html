Product & design on calls

demos, feedback rounds, defending a decision
Demo

Here’s the new checkout — let me walk you through the flow.

Sounds like a guided tour: you show it, then explain it.

instead of: I show you new page now

Feedback

Thanks, that’s helpful — I’ll fold it into the next round.

Takes criticism on board without sounding defensive.

instead of: OK, I change everything

Pushback

I hear you — here’s why I’d keep it as it is.

A polite “no”: acknowledge first, then explain.

instead of: No, my design is correct

Engineering on calls

estimates, trade-offs, explaining tech to non-technical people
Estimate

It’s doable, but I’d need two more days to cover edge cases.

A yes with a clear condition and a number attached.

instead of: Is impossible to do to Friday

Incident

It times out under load, so I’m adding a cache.

Names the cause and the next step in one breath.

instead of: Server is slow, I do something

In plain terms

In plain terms: the service was waiting on the database.

Puts a technical cause in words anyone can follow.

instead of: The query have deadlock

Analytics & QA on calls

presenting findings, defending priorities, sounding factual instead of accusing
Finding

Conversion dipped 8% in March — here’s what the data shows.

Leads with the fact, then promises the evidence.

instead of: Conversion is going down, is bad

Priority

It isn’t a blocker, but I’d fix it before the launch.

Ranks the problem without raising the temperature.

instead of: This is very bad, you must fix

Checking

Just to confirm: is this the expected behavior?

Asks the question without sounding like a challenge.

instead of: This is correct or not?

Operations on calls

incidents, handoffs, “why was the shipment late?”
Hold

Shipments are on hold until the carrier confirms the pickup.

States the status and the condition for moving on.

instead of: We not send now, carrier red

Incident

We rolled back at 2:05 p.m.; the root cause is still under investigation.

Time, action, status: three facts, no guessing.

instead of: We don’t know why it falled

Post-mortem

Let’s keep the review blameless — just the facts and next steps.

Moves the room from blame to fixes.

instead of: Who did this problem?

Ask a question
01What English level do I need?+
Intermediate (B1) or higher. If you can read emails and documents in English and follow a meeting when people speak slowly, you’re set. This isn’t a beginner course: we don’t cover the alphabet or basic tenses.
02How much time does it take?+
Two lessons a week of 30–40 minutes, plus 10–15 minutes of speaking practice a day. That’s 2–3 hours a week, easy to fit around work.
03What if I miss the Friday review?+
Every review is recorded. Send your own recordings by Thursday and your coach will go through them, even if you watch the session later.
04How is this different from regular English courses?+
Only real workplace situations: team syncs, feedback, incidents, client calls, job interviews. No chapters about the weather or holidays.
05Will I get a certificate?+
No. Instead you finish with a mock job interview, and you’ll know exactly where you stand.
06When does the next cohort start?+
A new cohort begins every month. Send an application and we’ll email you the start date and answer your questions before you pay.
